Connection Odds
30 US hubs / Sep 2023 to Aug 2026

Missed connections, built from flight records

A 45-minute connection was missed about one time in six

Across the 30 busiest US airports, 17% of 45-minute connections failed between September 2023 and August 2026, counting cancellations. At Atlanta it was 14%, and a 95% chance took 130 minutes. Judging the risk from the inbound flight alone overstated it by 2.8 points at 45 minutes, because the outbound flight tends to be late on the same days. The numbers come from 23.0 million domestic flights recorded by the US Bureau of Transportation Statistics, paired into 90.0 million connections.

Method

Connections are built from flight pairs, not bookings

The BTS on-time table lists flights, not passengers. Every connection here is constructed. At each hub, an arriving flight is paired with every departure on the same marketing airline (the name on the ticket, so a SkyWest-operated United Express leg counts as United) that leaves 30 to 180 minutes later on the schedule and goes somewhere other than where the arrival came from. Pairs flown by the same aircraft are dropped, since that plane cannot leave without its own passengers; that removed 1.5% of 567 million candidates, up to 8.6% at TPA. To keep every arrival equally represented, one departure is drawn at random for each arrival in each 15-minute layover band, leaving 90.0 million pairs on 196 hub and airline combinations.

A pair is missed if the inbound was cancelled or diverted, if the outbound was cancelled, or if the inbound reached the gate too late: actual arrival plus a walk of W minutes later than actual departure minus D minutes, where D is when the door closes. The defaults are W = 20 and D = 10, so a connection needs 30 minutes between gate arrival and pushback. The lookup tool lets you change W.

Curves

The first half hour of extra layover does most of the work

The miss rate falls from 31% at 30 minutes to 12% at an hour and 6% at two hours, then flattens toward the cancellation floor. The worst hub at 45 minutes was Washington National at 23%; the best was Baltimore at 12%.

The 95% layover

Evening arrivals need longer layovers

Departures leaving 15 or more minutes late rise from 7% in the 5 am hour to 32% at 19:00. Counting cancellations, only 22 of the 30 hubs reach a 95% chance within 180 minutes at all, so the map below takes cancellations out and asks a narrower question: if both flights operate, how much layover does a 95% chance need? At the median hub it is 60 minutes for an 8 am arrival and 120 for a 7 pm one.

The shortcut

The outbound flight is late on the same days

The obvious way to judge a connection is to ask how often the inbound flight lands more than the spare time late. That ignores the outbound. When the inbound was 30 or more minutes late, the outbound left 15 or more minutes late 40% of the time, against 24% of all pairs: the same storm and the same congested airport hold up both. The correlation between the two delays is 0.15 overall, from 0.05 at SLC to 0.21 at EWR.

At 45 minutes the shortcut gives 20.0% against a paired 17.2%. The delay part alone overstates by 3.4 points (17.6% against 14.2%), offset a little by the 1.1 points of outbound cancellations the shortcut never sees. The gap closes at about 120 minutes and reverses after it, when cancellations make up most of the remaining risk. By hub it runs from -0.3 points at SLC to 5.7 at BWI.

Evaluation

Three estimators, tested on six months they never saw

Each estimator was fitted on September 2023 to February 2026 and scored on 2,000,000 pairs from March to August 2026, where 11.0% were missed. The inbound-only shortcut uses the same cells and backoff as the cell average, so the two differ only in what they average. The logistic model has terms for hub and airline, layover, arrival hour, month and weekday, with layover, month and hour allowed to vary by hub; it was fitted on 3,000,000 training pairs.

The model calibrated best by a small margin over the cell average, and the tool ships it. Both predicted fewer misses than happened in the test months (10.5% and 10.4% against 11.0%): March to August 2026 was worse than the same months before it (11.0% missed, against 10.4% in 2024 and 10.5% in 2025), and pairs lost to a cancelled or diverted flight rose to 3.8% from about 3.1%. The shortcut was the worst calibrated; in its top tenth it predicted 37% and saw 28%. Brier skill against always predicting the test average is small for all three, about 5.9% at best: most of what decides a single connection, such as a thunderstorm that afternoon, is not in the inputs.

To rank what drives risk, each factor was dropped from the model in turn and the test log loss compared. The layover itself mattered most, then the hour of arrival and the hub and airline. Dropping the month made the test score slightly better: which months are bad changes from year to year.

Pre-pandemic

2019 looked much the same

The same 30 hubs and airlines in calendar 2019 (32.0 million pairs) missed 17% of 45-minute and 12% of 60-minute connections, against 17% and 12% in the main window. Cancellations and diversions were higher, 3.7% against 3.0%, so 95% was not reached within 180 minutes; if both flights flew, it took 90 minutes. Hub by hub at 45 minutes, 18 of 30 are worse now. DCA rose most (4.8 points) and EWR fell most (7.2 points).

Limits

What this cannot tell you

  • Constructed pairs are not real bookings. Airlines do not sell every pair, and each sets a minimum connection time per airport. No public table of those minimums was found, so the tool greys layovers under 35 minutes rather than using each airline's figure.
  • One walking time per connection is a simplification. A gate change across a big airport can take much longer than 20 minutes, which is why W is a slider.
  • Only US domestic flights by the carriers that report to BTS. No international legs, no customs, no partner-airline connections.
  • Airlines sometimes hold a departure for connecting passengers. That appears here as outbound delay and cannot be told apart from ordinary delay.
  • A miss here means rebooking. How many hours it cost is not measured.
  • Pairs share arrivals and days, so the intervals shown are too narrow.
